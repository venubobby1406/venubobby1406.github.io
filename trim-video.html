02 — First, the honest question

Before you use it, it is fair to ask whether you need it at all.

Do you need to re-encode at all?

Purists will tell you a trim should be lossless — cut the container, leave the frames alone. Real editors do that. A browser can't: to cut precisely, we decode the frames, drop the ones you don't want, and re-encode what's left.

For trimming the dead air off a screen recording, that's completely fine. For frame-perfect cutting of film footage, use an editor that trims without re-encoding. The bench works either way — we're not going to pretend a web page is DaVinci.

03 — When not to use this

Every tool has a job it is bad at. Here is this one's.

  • you're cutting film or high-bitrate footage → use an editor that trims losslessly
  • you need to cut mid-video and keep both halves → this page keeps one span; run it twice from the original
  • you only need a sound bite → extract the audio, don't re-render video you'll delete
  • you're on a laptop about to sleep → keep the tab awake; the render is the playback
04 — Questions people actually ask

Asked, answered, no padding.

Does it upload my file?
No. The bench is JavaScript running in this tab. Your recording touches nothing but your own machine — you can even go offline after the page loads and it still works.
Why does it take as long as the clip?
Because the conversion is the playback — every frame is decoded and re-encoded in real time. A server farm would be faster and would also mean your recording leaves your machine.
Is it really free?
Everything on this site is free forever. The plan is one quiet sponsor slot and ads we'd be embarrassed by — never your files, never your data. The about page has the receipts.
