02 — First, the honest question

Do you need this at all?

Do you actually want a GIF?

If it's going somewhere a player lives — YouTube, a course page, a file share — send the MP4 or a short clip. Players pause, and they have sound.

A GIF earns its place when there's no player: a chat box, a bug ticket, a slide, a README. It moves, it loops, it needs nothing installed.

03 — When not to use this

Every tool has a job it is bad at. Here is this one's.

  • it has sound that matters → a GIF is silent; use a video or a WAV
  • it's longer than a moment → trim to the 8 seconds that carry the point
  • it's smooth footage → 10 fps looks choppy; that's the format, not a bug
  • it needs to stay crisp → 256 colours will band a gradient. It's a GIF.
04 — Questions people actually ask

Asked, answered.

Is it really free?
Yes — funded by one quiet sponsor slot, never by your files or your data.
Does it upload my file?
No. The bench is JavaScript in this tab — your file touches nothing but your own machine.
Why is my GIF so big?
GIF stores every frame with no compression between them. Shorter range, smaller width: eight seconds at 480px is a friendly GIF; thirty seconds at 1080p is a monster.
