02 — First, the honest question

Do you need this at all?

Do you even need the swap?

If the file is going back to a browser — Gmail preview, a course page, a chat, an embed — stop here. Browsers have played WebM natively for a decade.

You need MP4 only when software demands it: Premiere, Final Cut, DaVinci, iMovie, CapCut, some phone photo apps.

03 — When not to use this

Every tool has a job it is bad at. Here is this one's.

  • it's staying online → send the WebM; browsers read it fine
  • you only need a moment of it → trim first, convert less
  • it's headed for a chat box → a GIF or a short clip, straight from the WebM
  • you're on Firefox → this page can't produce MP4 there. It will tell you, not pretend.
04 — Questions people actually ask

Asked, answered.

Is it really free?
Yes — funded by one quiet sponsor slot, never by your files or your data.
Does it upload my file?
No. The bench is JavaScript in this tab — your file touches nothing but your own machine.
Why does it take as long as the video?
Because it decodes every frame and hands it to the encoder as it plays. A server farm would be faster — and would also mean your recording leaves your machine.
